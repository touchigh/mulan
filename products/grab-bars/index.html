Bathroom grab bars and armrests, including luminous night versions

L-shaped bars, flip-up toilet armrests and washbasin rails with anti-slip grips.

WhatsApp us

In one line

MULAN makes L-shaped grab bars, U-shaped and side flip-up toilet armrests and washbasin armrests. Grips use raised anti-slip particles; one L-shaped bar has a 36 mm resin tube over an aluminium alloy core. Luminous versions have rings that glow at night after absorbing light.

Who it's for

  • Homes adding support beside the toilet, shower and basin
  • Care homes standardising handrails across rooms
  • Distributors needing a full grab-bar range

Problems it solves

  • Bars that are cold, slippery or turn in the hand
  • Fixed rails that block wheelchair transfers
  • Bars that are hard to find at night

Features

  • L-shaped grab bar

    36 mm resin outer tube with aluminium alloy core; card-slot anti-rotation.

  • U-shaped flip-up armrest

    Reinforced metal keel; folds up; anti-slip nylon particles.

  • Side flip-up armrest

    Thickened steel plate base.

  • Washbasin armrest

    Thermostatic anti-slip sleeve over stainless steel.

  • Luminous versions

    Rings glow after absorbing light.

  • Colours

    White; several models also in yellow.

Frequently asked questions

Which grab bars does MULAN make?

MULAN makes L-shaped grab bars in several materials, U-shaped and side flip-up armrests for beside the toilet, and armrests that frame a washbasin. Most come in white, and several are also available in yellow for higher contrast.

Are the grab bars easy to find at night?

The luminous versions have glowing rings that light up in the dark after absorbing light, so the bar is easier to find at night. The note in the catalogue says they only glow after absorbing a light source.

Will the bar turn or slip in the hand?

Grips have raised anti-slip particles. The 36 mm L-shaped bar uses a resin outer tube over an aluminium alloy tube and a card-slot design that stops it rotating.

Why choose a flip-up armrest beside the toilet?

A flip-up armrest folds up out of the way, which leaves room for a wheelchair transfer or a carer, then folds down for support when the user sits or stands.
