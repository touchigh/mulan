Chinese New Year 2027: factory shutdown and order deadlines

Updated 04 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · MULAN editorial team

In short

Chinese New Year 2027 falls on Saturday 6 February, and Lunar New Year's Eve on 5 February is a statutory holiday. Factories slow down before the break and restart gradually after it. Confirm your supplier's exact shutdown and restart dates now, approve samples in November, place orders in December and aim to finish loading by late January.

Chinese New Year 2027 falls on Saturday 6 February, starting the Year of the Goat, and the festival season closes with the Lantern Festival on 20 February (2027 date). The official holiday schedule for 2027 is set by China's State Council, which usually publishes the next year's arrangement in early November. Last year's notice came out on 4 November 2025 and gave a nine-day Spring Festival break from 15 to 23 February 2026 (2026 notice). If you buy bathroom products from China for delivery in the first quarter of 2027, now is the time to plan.

Why does Chinese New Year affect orders for so long?

The statutory holiday is only part of the story. Since 1 January 2025, Lunar New Year's Eve has been a statutory holiday, extending the Spring Festival break by a day (holiday rules). Around that fixed holiday, three things stretch the real disruption:

  • Workers leave early. Many factory staff travel long distances home, so output often drops in the days before the official break.
  • Restart is gradual. After the holiday, it takes time for teams to return and for material suppliers to restart, so full output can lag the official return date.
  • Everyone ships at once. Buyers rush to load goods before the break, which puts pressure on production slots, inspection bookings, trucking and ports.

Each factory sets its own closing and reopening dates. The only way to plan reliably is to ask your supplier for its dates in writing.

What does a late order look like in practice?

A distributor in Kuwait has a care-home project that must open in March 2027. It needs grab bars for every bathroom: L-shaped bars beside the toilets, flip-up arms where wheelchair users transfer, and washbasin rails. The buyer sends the purchase order in mid-January, assuming four weeks of production is enough. The factory's material suppliers are already closing, the production calendar is full of orders placed in December, and the goods cannot load until after the holiday. The project opening slips by a month. The same order placed in early December would have shipped in January.

Which steps reduce the risk of a missed deadline?

  1. Ask for dates in writing now. Request the factory's last production day, last loading day and restart date for 2027.
  2. Freeze the specification early. Changes to colour, packaging or labels in January are the most common reason orders miss the pre-holiday window.
  3. Split the order. Ship standard, high-volume items such as grab bars before the holiday and schedule complex items for after it.
  4. Book inspection and freight early. Inspection agencies and forwarders are busiest in the last weeks before the break.
  5. Prepare documents in advance. Have commercial invoice, packing list and any certificates your market needs ready before loading, so customs paperwork does not wait for staff to return.
  6. Agree a post-holiday plan. Ask when production of your next order can restart and book the slot before the holiday.

Which products are easiest to ship before the break?

Standard catalogue items in standard finishes move fastest, because materials and packaging are already defined. Grab bars and handrails are usually in this group, and they are also the items most projects need in the largest quantities. Bulky plumbed or electrical products, such as seated showers or powered toilet aids, need more checking and more container space, so order them first or plan them for after the holiday. What is the short version?

Chinese New Year falls on 6 February 2027, and the real disruption runs from mid-January to late February. Confirm your supplier's dates now, sign off samples in November, order in early December, check production on 12 January and aim to load by 26 January. Anything later should be planned as a post-holiday shipment.

Frequently asked questions

When is Chinese New Year 2027?

Chinese New Year 2027 falls on Saturday 6 February 2027 and begins the Year of the Goat. Lunar New Year's Eve, on 5 February, has been a statutory holiday in China since 2025. The festival season ends with the Lantern Festival on 20 February 2027. The exact days off and make-up working days are set each year by the State Council.

How long do Chinese factories close for Chinese New Year?

The official Spring Festival break is usually around eight or nine days once weekends and make-up working days are arranged; in 2026 it ran for nine days. Many factories close earlier and restart later than the official dates, because workers travel home and suppliers reopen gradually. Each factory sets its own schedule, so ask your supplier for its dates in writing.

When should I place orders to ship before Chinese New Year 2027?

As a planning rule, approve samples by late November 2026 and place purchase orders in early December, so production can finish and goods can be inspected and loaded by late January 2027. Orders with custom packaging, new colours or certification work need more margin. Replace these checkpoints with the dates your supplier confirms.

Has China published the official 2027 holiday schedule?

The State Council normally announces the next year's public holiday arrangement in early November; the 2026 schedule was issued on 4 November 2025. Check the Chinese government's official notice for the 2027 dates, then confirm how your own supplier will apply them, since factory closing and restart dates often differ from the official holiday.
